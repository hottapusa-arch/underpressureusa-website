Aging Infrastructure & Replacement

Why age alone does not tell the whole story.

← All Infrastructure Failures topics

What is happening?

Many communities operate networks built over multiple generations. Different materials, installation practices, and operating conditions can exist on the same street.

Why it happens

Some older pipes remain in good condition, while newer sections may fail early. Utilities weigh break history, consequences of failure, condition, critical customers, and available funding.

What crews look for

Repeated failures in one area can signal a need for more than spot repairs, but replacing every old pipe immediately is rarely practical.

What we can learn

Risk-based asset management helps prioritize inspections, maintenance, rehabilitation, and capital projects.

What matters in the field

Age alone is not a failure diagnosis. Utilities weigh break history, material, soil conditions, consequence of failure, and the number of customers served when deciding what to replace first.

A practical example

A main that has never broken may still be a high priority if it serves a hospital and has no alternate feed.

Age is only part of the story

A pipe installed decades ago may still have years of useful life, while a newer main can fail because of aggressive soil, poor bedding, pressure cycling, or installation damage. Utilities combine break history, consequence of failure, material, soil conditions, and customer impact when deciding what to replace first.
