Asbestos Cement Pipe

Asbestos cement pipe was widely installed in twentieth-century water systems and is still encountered during repairs.

← All pipe materials

Where it came from

The material combined cement with asbestos fibers to produce rigid pipe sections.

Why it was used

It offered a relatively smooth interior and resistance to some forms of corrosion.

What can go wrong

Aging, breakage, and cutting or disturbance require special attention. Work that may release asbestos fibers must follow applicable safety and regulatory requirements.

What the history teaches us

Legacy materials deserve accurate identification and a planned, compliant approach.

What matters in the field

Asbestos-cement pipe was used in water distribution for decades. Age, soil movement, joint condition, and previous repairs all affect how an existing main behaves.

A practical example

Disturbing asbestos-containing material can create an exposure hazard. Applicable asbestos rules and qualified procedures govern cutting, removal, and disposal.

What crews encounter today

Asbestos-cement water mains can be brittle, especially after years of loading and soil movement. Their outside diameters and condition must be verified before selecting repair fittings. Disturbing asbestos-containing material can create occupational exposure hazards, so applicable regulations and approved handling procedures matter.
