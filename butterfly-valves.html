Butterfly Valves

Compact quarter-turn valves frequently used on larger water mains.

← Explore all valves & fittings

How they work

A disc rotates inside the valve body. Its position changes the available opening and can shut off flow when seated.

Where they fit

Butterfly valves are common where size, weight, and installation space matter, including large-diameter transmission and distribution applications.

What to watch

Disc clearance, actuator condition, seating surfaces, and pressure differential affect performance. The disc remains in the flow path when open.

Why it matters

The right valve selection depends on service conditions and manufacturer ratings, not diameter alone.

What matters in the field

A butterfly valve controls flow with a disc rotating inside the waterway. This makes it compact, but the disc remains in the flow path even when the valve is open.

A practical example

Because the disc occupies the bore, a conventional full-bore hot-tapping cutter cannot simply pass through a butterfly valve.

Where butterfly valves fit

A butterfly valve rotates a disc inside the flow path, usually through a quarter turn. Large sizes can be compact and economical, but the disc remains in the waterway even when fully open. That makes valve selection important for work requiring unobstructed access, such as many tapping operations.
