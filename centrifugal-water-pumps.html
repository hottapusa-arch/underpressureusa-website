Centrifugal Water Pumps

Centrifugal pumps are widely used to move water through treatment plants and distribution systems.

← All Pumps & Pressure topics

How a centrifugal pump moves water

A rotating impeller transfers energy to water entering near its center. The casing guides the flow toward discharge. The pump adds hydraulic head; the resulting pressure and flow depend on the connected system.

Main components

The impeller and casing form the hydraulic passage. The shaft transmits rotation, bearings support the assembly, and seals limit leakage around the shaft. Motors and controls supply and regulate the drive.

Head, pressure, and flow

Pump head is energy per unit weight of liquid, commonly expressed as feet of water. A pressure gauge measures local pressure. For water, about 2.31 feet of head equals 1 psi, but pressure at any location also depends on elevation and losses.

Why the operating point matters

The pump curve describes available head across flow rates; the system curve describes the head the piping requires. Their intersection determines the operating point. Closing a valve, changing tank level, or altering pipe resistance can change that point.

Warning signs

Unusual vibration, cavitation-like noise, overheating, loss of capacity, or repeated seal trouble can indicate a problem. Causes vary, so diagnosis should include suction conditions, operating point, air entry, and equipment condition.
