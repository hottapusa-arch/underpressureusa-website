Check Valves

One-way flow protection in pumping and distribution applications.

← Explore all valves & fittings

A valve that responds to flow

A check valve is designed to permit flow in its intended direction and limit reverse flow. Unlike a hand-operated isolation valve, it normally opens and closes because of the flow and pressure conditions acting on its internal parts. It is not a substitute for a positive isolation plan where a verified shutoff is required.

What is inside

A swing check has a hinged disc that moves away from its seat as forward flow develops. Other designs use a guided disc, spring, ball, or other closing element. The body contains the flow path and seat. Different designs respond at different speeds and create different amounts of resistance, so two check valves of the same nominal size may behave differently.

Why pumps often need them

When a pump stops, the water in the discharge line may still have momentum, and elevation or pressure elsewhere in the system may try to drive water backward. A correctly selected check valve helps prevent that reverse movement. Its location and behavior are part of the pump-station design, not just a convenient fitting choice.

What valve slam means

A check valve can close abruptly after flow reverses or changes rapidly. That closing action may create a pressure transient and a loud impact. The event is often called check-valve slam. Not every banging sound is caused by a check valve, and replacing one without understanding the flow conditions may leave the real problem unresolved.

What can go wrong

Debris on a seat can prevent tight closure. Wear, damaged hinges or springs, incorrect installation orientation, and unsuitable flow conditions can affect operation. A valve that passes water backward may be leaking, but the correct diagnosis requires understanding the connected system and the specific valve design.

The bigger lesson

A check valve protects against a particular direction of flow; it does not control the whole system. Pump shutdown, trapped air, and water hammer are connected subjects. Our Air & Water Hammer Fundamentals guide explores how moving water and changing pressure can affect valves and piping.
