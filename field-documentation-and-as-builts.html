Field Documentation and As-Builts

Why good notes and measurements are part of the repair.

← All Field Operations topics

The next crew needs answers

Accurate records help future crews find valves, fittings, repairs, and service connections. A repair that is not documented can become a problem years later.

Capture what changed

Useful records may include pipe material, diameter, fitting type, measurements from fixed landmarks, photographs, and the location of installed components.

Maps improve over time

GIS and asset-management systems are only as reliable as the field information entered into them. Updates after each job make the system more useful.

Knowledge that stays

Good documentation preserves lessons from experienced crews and reduces the amount of guessing required during the next emergency.

What matters in the field

Accurate records should capture what was actually installed, not only what was drawn before construction. Location, depth, material, size, and connection details are valuable.

A practical example

A future crew may rely on today's measurements when deciding where to dig or which valve to close.

Record what was actually installed

A drawing may show the planned alignment, but field conditions often change. Good as-builts record actual locations, depths where measured, valve and fitting details, material transitions, and relevant photographs. That information can save hours during the next emergency.
