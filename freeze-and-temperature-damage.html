Freeze & Temperature Damage

Cold weather can affect more than exposed plumbing.

← All Infrastructure Failures topics

What is happening?

Freezing water expands and can damage exposed equipment, services, valves, and shallow infrastructure. Temperature changes can also affect ground movement and pipe stress.

Why it happens

Depth, insulation, water movement, weather duration, and local conditions all matter. A buried main below local frost depth faces different exposure than an aboveground bypass.

What crews look for

Crews check vulnerable exposed piping, hydrants, small-diameter services, and temporary equipment after severe weather.

What we can learn

Winter preparation includes protecting exposed components, monitoring conditions, and planning for safe restoration if something freezes.

What matters in the field

Water expands as it freezes. Exposed service lines, hydrant components, shallow piping, and stagnant sections can be vulnerable.

A practical example

A frozen section may not leak until thawing reveals a split or damaged joint.

Exposed components are often the weak point

Buried mains may be protected by soil cover, while hydrants, meters, temporary bypasses, and aboveground piping are exposed to freezing. Expanding ice can damage fittings or trap pressure between closed valves. Cold-weather planning must consider the entire temporary and permanent arrangement.
