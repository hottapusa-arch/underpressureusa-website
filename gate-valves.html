Gate Valves

A familiar isolation valve found throughout water distribution systems.

← Explore all valves & fittings

How they work

A gate moves into or out of the flow path. When fully open, many gate-valve designs provide a relatively unobstructed passage.

Where they fit

Utilities often use gate valves on distribution mains and branches to isolate sections for repairs and maintenance.

Limits and failures

Stems, seats, operating nuts, and internal components can wear or seize. A valve that turns may still allow water to pass.

Field perspective

Record the direction, turns, and observed response under an approved utility procedure; do not assume a valve seals simply because the operator reaches a stop.

What matters in the field

A gate valve typically raises or lowers a gate to isolate flow. It is generally intended for full-open or full-closed service, not routine throttling.

A practical example

A buried valve can turn without achieving tight shutoff, so operation count alone does not prove isolation.

Why gate valves are used for isolation

A gate valve lifts a wedge or gate out of the flow path, providing a relatively unobstructed opening when fully open. Most distribution gate valves are intended for fully open or fully closed service, not fine throttling. Seat condition, operating torque, and whether the valve actually seals are field concerns.
