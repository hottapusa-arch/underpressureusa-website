Gravity Sewer Mains

The sloped underground pipes that carry wastewater without continuous pumping.

← All Sewer Systems topics

Moving wastewater downhill

Gravity sewers use elevation difference to carry wastewater toward larger collectors, lift stations, or treatment facilities. They normally run partly full, with air space above the flow. Grade, diameter, roughness, and flow all affect how well they perform.

Slope is only part of the story

Too little velocity can let solids settle, but a steep grade does not guarantee trouble-free operation. A belly, offset joint, downstream restriction, or poorly installed service connection can create a backup even when much of the main has the intended slope.

Materials and installation

Gravity mains may be made of PVC, vitrified clay, concrete, ductile iron, HDPE, and other materials. Pipe stiffness or structural class, bedding, cover, joint tightness, and soil conditions are important. Pressure rating alone does not describe suitability for ordinary gravity service.

What crews commonly find

Grease, wipes, roots, sediment, cracked pipe, separated joints, and deformation can restrict flow or allow groundwater in. A boring or excavation strike can damage a sewer without producing an obvious surface leak, making inspection important after a suspected hit.

Inspection and maintenance

Cleaning and CCTV inspection help crews locate deposits and visible defects. Manhole observations and flow records add context. A camera shows the inside surface, but may not reveal soil loss or the full condition of the pipe wall.

Field takeaway

When a sewer backs up, investigate both the location of the obstruction and the downstream conditions. Repeated cleaning at the same spot may indicate an underlying grade, alignment, or structural problem that needs repair.

Educational overview only. Utility policies, site conditions, safety procedures, and applicable regulations govern field work.
