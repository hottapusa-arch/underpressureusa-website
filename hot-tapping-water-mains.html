Hot Tapping Water Mains

How crews create a new connection to an existing main while it remains in service.

← All Field Operations topics

What hot tapping actually does

Hot tapping means making a connection to an existing water main while the main remains in service. The term covers more than one method. Small service connections may be made directly into suitable pipe or through a tapping saddle. Larger branch connections commonly use a tapping sleeve, a purpose-built tapping gate valve, and a tapping machine. The goal is to make the connection while controlling the water already under pressure in the pipe.

Three common ways to make a tap

A direct tap is made into a pipe approved for that type of connection, typically for a small service. A saddle tap uses a compatible tapping saddle around the main to provide a connection and sealing arrangement. A tapping-sleeve tap generally serves a larger branch and uses a sleeve, tapping gate valve, and tapping machine. The choice depends on the pipe material and condition, connection size, utility standards, and the fitting manufacturer’s requirements.

Nominal tap size versus cutter size

The size used to describe a tap is generally the nominal size of the new connection, not the exact diameter of the hole cut in the existing main. An “8-inch hot tap” may use a cutter roughly half an inch smaller than the nominal connection size, but cutter diameter is not a universal fixed offset. The approved fitting, valve, cutter, and equipment determine the actual opening.

Why the existing pipe matters

The main must be assessed before work begins. Pipe material, outside diameter, wall condition, available working room, operating pressure, and the proposed connection size all affect whether the tap is suitable. A pipe that looks sound from the excavation may still have corrosion, deterioration, or damage that changes the plan.

Why a tapping valve is different

For a conventional larger tapping-sleeve installation, the valve is a tapping gate valve designed to provide a clear passage for the cutter when fully open. On common compatible flanged assemblies, the male alignment ring on the tapping valve flange mates with the female recess on the tapping sleeve flange. This helps center the valve and cutter over the sleeve outlet. The sleeve and valve must be designed to work together; flange appearance alone does not establish compatibility.

One assembly, several places a leak can occur

On a tapping-sleeve installation, the sleeve and tapping valve are pressure-tested together as one assembly before cutting, under the approved procedure. The test checks the sleeve-to-pipe seal, the sleeve-to-valve connection, and the pressure-containing parts and seals of the valve. Testing only the sleeve does not verify the complete assembly. The test method and pressure depend on the equipment and utility requirements.

What happens during the cut

The tapping machine is mounted to the tapping gate valve. With the valve fully open, the cutter passes through its unobstructed opening and advances into the pipe while the machine contains system pressure. A conventional butterfly valve is not used in this arrangement because its disc obstructs the cutter path. The cut section, called the coupon, must be retained and accounted for. After the cutter retracts behind the valve, the tapping valve is closed and the machine can be removed. The crew then follows the utility’s commissioning requirements.

Why a hot tap is not always the right choice

A hot tap can reduce customer outages, but it does not eliminate risk. Pipe condition, service interruptions during other work, traffic and excavation conditions, and the consequences of a failed connection all matter. A shutdown or alternate method may be safer when conditions do not support a reliable tap.

A question worth asking

When someone describes an “8-inch hot tap,” ask whether they mean the nominal connection size or the actual cutter diameter. Those are not the same measurement. And on a tapping-sleeve job, ask whether the tapping valve was attached when the assembly was tested.

A useful distinction

A hot tap creates a new connection to an operating main; it does not by itself isolate the flow inside that main. The sleeve, tapping gate valve, and machine form a temporary pressure-containing assembly while the opening is made. The finished connection remains subject to the utility’s approved design and testing requirements.
