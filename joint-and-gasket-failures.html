Joint & Gasket Failures

The pipe can be sound while the connection leaks.

← All Infrastructure Failures topics

What is happening?

Every pipe system depends on connections. Gaskets, mechanical joints, restrained joints, flanges, and couplings all have specific limits and installation requirements.

Why it happens

Misalignment, movement, poor seating, damaged gasket surfaces, incorrect assembly, and pressure changes can contribute to leakage. The right joint depends on pipe type and service conditions.

What crews look for

Crews check where the water is escaping and whether the joint has shifted or lost restraint. Water traveling along a pipe can make the visible wet spot misleading.

What we can learn

The repair must address the connection and any movement or restraint issue, not just the escaping water.

What matters in the field

A joint can fail because of movement, pressure, installation problems, incompatible dimensions, aging components, or loss of restraint.

A practical example

Replacing a gasket without addressing pipe movement may allow the same problem to return.

The pipe barrel may be fine

Many leaks occur at joints rather than through the pipe wall. Movement, inadequate insertion, gasket damage, pressure thrust, or poor restraint can compromise a connection. The failure location helps determine whether a local repair is enough or the underlying installation issue must be addressed.
