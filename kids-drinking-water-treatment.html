How Does Water Get Clean?

Mia looked at the water in Riverton's reservoir and then at the clear water in her drinking glass.

“How does it get so clean?”

Drip explained that water often goes through several important steps before reaching our homes.

Welcome to the Water Treatment Plant

A water treatment plant is a special facility where water is cleaned and prepared for drinking.

Different treatment plants use different methods depending on where their water comes from and what's in it.

Gathering the Tiny Particles

Water from rivers and lakes can contain tiny pieces of dirt and other materials.

At many treatment plants, special substances help these tiny particles stick together, forming larger clumps that are easier to remove.

Many of these clumps settle to the bottom of large tanks.

Filtering the Water

Water can then travel through special filters that help remove smaller particles.

Some filters contain layers of sand and other materials.

Bolt finds it amazing that sand can actually help clean water!

Making Water Safe

Water treatment plants also use processes called disinfection to kill or inactivate harmful germs.

Water professionals regularly test the water to make sure it meets drinking water safety standards.

Ready for the Journey

Once the water has been treated, it can travel through pumps, storage tanks and underground pipes to reach homes throughout Riverton.

Did You Know?

Water can look perfectly clear and still contain things that are too small for our eyes to see. That's why water treatment and testing are so important!

Your Next Discovery

The next time you fill a glass of water, think about all the people, equipment and work that helped get it there.
