Meet the Excavator

Max heard a familiar rumble coming from a construction site in Riverton.

A giant yellow machine was digging into the ground and lifting buckets full of dirt.

“That's an excavator!” Bolt announced.

Built for Digging

An excavator is a powerful machine that helps workers dig holes, uncover underground pipes and move heavy materials.

Instead of digging everything by hand, workers can use an excavator to move large amounts of soil much more quickly.

Meet the Moving Parts

An excavator has several important parts:

• **Boom:** The large section that extends from the machine.

• **Stick:** The section that connects the boom to the bucket.

• **Bucket:** Scoops and lifts soil.

• **Cab:** Where the operator sits and controls the machine.

• **Tracks:** Help the excavator move across uneven ground.

Many excavators can also spin completely around without moving their tracks!

The Power of Hydraulics

Excavators use a special system called hydraulics.

Pressurized oil moves through hoses and cylinders, giving the machine the strength to dig, lift and move heavy objects.

Bolt loves learning how hydraulics turn small movements of the controls into powerful movements of the machine.

More Than Just Digging

Workers can replace the bucket with different attachments to break concrete, lift materials and perform other jobs.

Excavators come in many sizes, from small machines that fit in backyards to enormous machines used in mines.

Did You Know?

Some excavators weigh more than 100 elephants combined!

Your Next Discovery

The next time you see an excavator, try to identify its boom, stick, bucket, cab and tracks.

Always watch from a safe distance and stay outside construction areas.

Keep exploring! There's an amazing world beneath your feet.
