How Do Fire Hydrants Work?

While walking through Riverton, Mia spotted a bright red fire hydrant.

“Is there water inside that thing?”

Drip explained that fire hydrants are connected to the same underground water system that supplies our homes!

Ready for an Emergency

Fire hydrants give firefighters access to large amounts of water when they need it.

Firefighters connect their hoses to the hydrant and use a special wrench to open it.

Water can then flow from the underground water main through the hydrant and into their hoses.

What's Beneath the Hydrant?

A fire hydrant is much bigger than the part you see above the ground.

Beneath many hydrants is a long barrel with a valve near the bottom.

In places where temperatures drop below freezing, this design allows water to drain from the hydrant after it's closed, helping prevent it from freezing.

Bolt thinks that's a pretty clever design!

Not Just for Fighting Fires

Water utility workers also use fire hydrants to flush water mains and test how much water the system can deliver.

Workers regularly inspect hydrants to make sure they're ready when needed.

Did You Know?

Some fire hydrants can deliver more than 1,000 gallons of water in just one minute!

That's enough to fill dozens of bathtubs!

Your Next Discovery

Look for fire hydrants around your neighborhood. What colors can you find?

Remember, fire hydrants need plenty of clear space around them so firefighters can reach them quickly.

Never try to open a fire hydrant yourself.

Keep exploring! There's an amazing world beneath your feet.
