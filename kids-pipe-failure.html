Why Do Water Pipes Break?

One morning, Mia noticed water bubbling up through a street in Riverton.

“But it hasn't even rained!”

Piper contacted Drip and Bolt to find out what was happening.

It looked like an underground water pipe had broken!

Pipes Don't Last Forever

Water pipes are built to last for many years, but just like roads, bridges and buildings, they can become damaged over time.

Some pipes beneath our streets are more than 100 years old!

What Causes Pipes to Break?

The ground surrounding a pipe can move as temperatures change or soil becomes wet and dry.

Some metal pipes can weaken because of corrosion, and sudden changes in water pressure can put extra stress on pipes.

Even newer pipes can sometimes break.

Where Does All That Water Go?

Water inside a drinking water pipe is under pressure.

When a pipe breaks, water can escape into the surrounding soil. Sometimes it finds its way to the surface, creating puddles or even flooding a street.

Water Heroes to the Rescue!

Utility workers locate the broken pipe, control the flow of water and make repairs.

Sometimes they work through the night, in the rain or even in the snow to get everyone's water flowing again.

Did You Know?

Not every broken water pipe creates a puddle. Some leaks stay hidden underground, making them much harder to find!

Your Next Discovery

If you ever notice water bubbling through a street or sidewalk, tell an adult. Stay away from the area so you can remain safe while utility workers investigate.

Keep exploring! There's an amazing world beneath your feet.
