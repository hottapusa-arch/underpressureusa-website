Where Does Rainwater Go?

After a big rainstorm in Riverton, Mia watched water rushing along the edge of the street and disappearing through a metal grate.

“Where is all that water going?”

Piper contacted Drip to investigate!

Rain Has to Go Somewhere

When rain falls on grass or soil, some of it soaks into the ground.

But when rain falls on streets, sidewalks, rooftops and parking lots, much of it flows across those surfaces.

This flowing water is called runoff.

Meet the Storm Drain

Storm drains help collect rainwater and carry it away from streets and other areas.

Underground pipes, ditches and channels help move the water toward ponds, streams and rivers.

These systems help reduce flooding throughout Riverton.

A Place to Slow Down

Some communities have special ponds that collect rainwater during storms.

These ponds help manage how quickly water flows into nearby streams and rivers.

You might have seen one near a shopping center or neighborhood!

Keeping Rainwater Clean

Rainwater can pick up litter, oil and other pollutants as it flows across streets and parking lots.

In many communities, stormwater flows into streams and rivers without going through a treatment plant.

That's why keeping our streets and neighborhoods clean is so important.

Did You Know?

One inch of rain falling on a 1,000-square-foot roof produces more than 600 gallons of water!

That's a lot of rainwater from just one roof.

Your Next Discovery

After the next rainstorm, look for storm drains and follow the path of rainwater with an adult from a safe location.

Never enter a storm drain, drainage pipe or flooded area.

Keep exploring! There's an amazing world beneath your feet.
