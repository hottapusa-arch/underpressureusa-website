How Water Pressure Works

Select the illustration to view it at full size.

Mia notices that homes in Riverton sit at different heights. Drip explains how elevation affects water pressure.

Height Makes a Difference

Water in a tower has weight. The greater the vertical distance below the water surface, the greater the pressure from that water column, if other conditions are the same.

Three Homes

A home 50 feet below the water surface would have about 22 PSI from elevation alone. At 100 feet it would be about 43 PSI, and at 150 feet about 65 PSI. These examples assume still water and no extra pressure from pumps.

Real Water Systems

Moving water loses some pressure through pipes and fittings. Pumps and pressure-control valves also affect pressure. Utility workers manage these differences so neighborhoods receive suitable water service.

Did You Know?

Each foot of water height produces about 0.433 PSI of pressure.
