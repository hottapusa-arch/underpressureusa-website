How Does a Wastewater Treatment Plant Work?

Mia wondered what happened to all the dirty water leaving the homes in Riverton.

“How do they clean something that dirty?”

Drip and Bolt were ready to explain!

The Big Cleanup

Every day, wastewater from homes, schools and businesses travels through sewer pipes to Riverton's wastewater treatment plant.

There, workers and special equipment help remove waste and other unwanted materials from the water.

Catching the Big Stuff

Wastewater passes through screens that catch things like wipes, trash and other large objects.

Special equipment also helps remove sand and grit that could damage the machinery.

Tiny Workers With a Big Job

Some of the most important helpers at a wastewater treatment plant are too small to see without a microscope!

Tiny living organisms called microorganisms help break down waste in the water.

Workers carefully manage the conditions these microorganisms need to do their job.

Finishing the Cleanup

Additional treatment helps remove remaining waste and reduce harmful germs.

Workers test the treated water to make sure it meets environmental requirements before it leaves the plant.

Returning Water to Nature

Treated wastewater may be returned to rivers or other bodies of water. Some communities reuse it for watering parks, golf courses and other areas.

Wastewater treatment helps protect our environment.

Did You Know?

A wastewater treatment plant works around the clock because people use water during the day and at night!

Your Next Discovery

You can help wastewater treatment workers by keeping trash, cooking grease and wipes out of your drains and toilets.

Small actions at home can make a big difference!
