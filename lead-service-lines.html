Lead Service Lines

Lead was once used for water service connections, but it is a serious drinking-water health concern.

← All pipe materials

Where it came from

Older service lines can contain lead between the water main and the building. Some buildings may also have other lead-bearing plumbing components.

Why it was used

Historically, lead was valued for workability; those past reasons do not outweigh the health risks now recognized.

What can go wrong

Lead can enter drinking water through corrosion. Utilities and property owners should consult local replacement programs and current public-health guidance.

What the history teaches us

The priority is identifying and replacing lead service lines, not preserving a legacy material.

What matters in the field

Lead service lines are a public-health concern because lead can enter drinking water. Replacement programs and corrosion control address different parts of the risk.

A practical example

The material of a water main does not prove the material of each individual service line connected to it.

The service line is part of the water system

Lead service lines can release lead into drinking water, especially under corrosive water conditions. Utilities identify and replace lead-containing connections under applicable regulations, while corrosion control remains important. Disturbing a service line can temporarily change lead levels, so replacement programs include customer communication and protective measures.
