Line Stopping Water Mains

How temporary isolation can make repairs possible without shutting down a large service area.

← All Field Operations topics

What is line stopping?

Line stopping is a way to temporarily isolate part of a water main without first shutting down the entire system. A specialized stopping head is introduced into the pressurized pipe through a fitting and controlled access opening. Once positioned, the head forms a barrier so crews can work on a selected section of main while other parts of the system may remain in service. It is temporary isolation, not a new permanent valve.

Why utilities use it

Imagine a water main that needs a damaged section replaced, but the existing valves would require shutting off water to a much larger area. A properly planned line stop may let the utility isolate only the section being repaired. That can reduce disruption to homes, businesses, and critical customers. The actual area affected depends on the pipe network, the available isolation points, and the job plan.

The line-stop fitting and sleeve

The work starts with a pressure-rated line-stop fitting installed around or onto the existing pipe. Depending on the system, it may be a full-encirclement sleeve or another engineered fitting. It creates a secure connection to the pipe and a controlled opening for the stopping equipment. Material, outside diameter, wall condition, operating pressure, and available space all matter. A nominal pipe size alone is not enough to select the fitting.

The temporary valve and tapping machine

A temporary tapping gate valve is commonly installed on the line-stop fitting. The tapping machine connects above it and makes the required opening while the pipe remains under pressure. After the cutter is withdrawn, the temporary valve can be closed so the tapping machine is removed and the line-stop equipment attached. As with hot tapping, the fitting and temporary valve must be evaluated and tested as a connected pressure-containing assembly under the applicable procedures.

The line-stop machine and stopping head

The line-stop machine moves the stopping head through the temporary valve and into the main. The head is designed to create a seal against the inside of the pipe. Different systems use different head designs, sealing elements, and methods of positioning them. The important idea is that the head must be suited to the actual pipe, pressure, and operating conditions. A line stop is not simply a plug pushed into a hole.

One stop, two stops, and bypasses

Some repairs need a stop on each side of the work area. Other projects can use an existing valve as one isolation point. A bypass line may also be installed to route water around the isolated section and maintain service where the network requires it. A bypass is not automatic: its need and capacity depend on demand, system configuration, pressure, and the customers being served.

How the temporary equipment comes off

After the planned work is complete and the system is ready to return to normal operation, the stopping head is withdrawn. In common line-stop systems, a completion plug is then installed in the fitting neck to provide the permanent pressure seal. The temporary valve and line-stop equipment can be removed, while the fitting and its approved closure remain on the main. The exact closure arrangement varies by manufacturer and fitting design.

Line stopping versus valve insertion

This is the biggest distinction: a line stop creates temporary isolation for a specific job. A valve insert installs a permanent operating valve that remains available for future system control. Both can involve sleeves, temporary valves, cutting equipment, and pressure-containing operations, but they serve different long-term purposes.

What makes a job challenging?

Older pipe may be out of round, corroded, lined, or different from what the plans show. Flow, pressure changes, available excavation space, nearby fittings, and the consequences of a failed isolation all influence the plan. A line stop is a specialized operation requiring qualified crews, approved equipment, and the manufacturer’s procedures. It is not suitable for every pipe or every situation.
