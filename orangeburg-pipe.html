Orangeburg Sewer Pipe

Orangeburg is a bituminous fiber pipe associated with older sewer laterals and drains.

← All pipe materials

Where it came from

Manufacturers formed fiber material with bituminous binders into lightweight pipe sections.

Why it was used

It was inexpensive and easy to handle for its time.

What can go wrong

Over time, some installations deform, blister, collapse, or become vulnerable to root intrusion. It is not equivalent to modern plastic sewer pipe.

What the history teaches us

An old sewer lateral can fail through gradual loss of shape, not just a sudden break.

What matters in the field

Orangeburg pipe is a bituminous-fiber sewer material used historically, especially for smaller lines. Deformation, delamination, and root intrusion can affect service.

A practical example

A sewer backup may come from a collapsed section, not simply something that can be cleared with cleaning equipment.

A material with a narrow history

Orangeburg pipe was made from layers of wood fiber bonded with a bituminous material and used in some sewer and drainage applications. It can deform or deteriorate with age and loading. Its presence is a reminder that a pipe’s service conditions matter as much as its original advertised benefits.
