Pipes & Materials

Before water reaches a faucet or wastewater reaches a treatment plant, it travels through miles of pipe. The materials used to build those systems have changed dramatically over time.

Explore how different pipe materials were developed, why they were used, and what decades of service have taught us about their strengths and weaknesses.

Explore the materials

From wooden pipes to modern plastics.

01 / WOOD

Wooden Pipe

Early water systems used hollowed logs and wooden stave pipes. Explore how they worked and why they disappeared.

02 / CAST IRON

Cast Iron

A major part of America's water infrastructure history, with some pipes remaining in service for more than a century.

03 / DUCTILE IRON

Ductile Iron

Developed to improve strength and toughness while retaining the benefits of iron pipe.

04 / PVC

PVC

A widely used modern pipe material. Learn about its pressure ratings, dimensions, installation, and common problems.

05 / ASBESTOS CEMENT

Asbestos Cement

Once commonly installed in water distribution systems, this material presents unique maintenance and handling concerns.

06 / HDPE

HDPE

Flexible polyethylene pipe known for its fusion joints and ability to accommodate movement.

07 / CONCRETE

PCCP & Concrete

Large-diameter pipe systems used for major water transmission and other infrastructure applications.

08 / STEEL

Steel Pipe

Used in large transmission mains, high-pressure applications, and custom infrastructure projects.

09 / ORANGEBURG

Orangeburg Pipe

A bituminous fiber pipe used in older sewer systems, known for deterioration and deformation over time.

10 / COPPER

Copper Pipe

Common in water services and plumbing, with a long history of use in drinking water systems.

11 / LEAD

Lead Pipe

Understand the history of lead service lines and why replacing them remains an important drinking water priority.

12 / EVOLUTION

The Evolution of Pipe

See how changing materials, manufacturing methods, and engineering practices shaped today's underground networks.
