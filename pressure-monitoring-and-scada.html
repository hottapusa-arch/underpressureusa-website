Pressure Monitoring and SCADA

Instrumentation and controls help utilities see how their systems are operating.

← All Pumps & Pressure topics

How it works

Pressure sensors, flow meters, tank-level instruments, and supervisory control systems provide operating data and alarms.

Why it matters

Operators can identify trends, respond to abnormal conditions, and coordinate pumps and valves.

What can go wrong?

Data quality, sensor calibration, communications, and cybersecurity are important to dependable monitoring.

Remember this

An alarm helps identify a condition; it does not replace field verification and operator judgment.

A reading needs context

SCADA can collect pressure, tank level, pump status, alarms, and trends across a utility system. A sudden pressure drop might indicate a main break, pump event, sensor problem, or normal demand change. Operators interpret multiple signals rather than relying on one alarm.

Explore the bigger system

Pressure, flow, pumps, storage, and valves all influence one another. See our overview of water distribution systems to understand how the pieces connect.

Educational overview only. System-specific design, operation, and maintenance require applicable standards, approved procedures, and qualified personnel.

What matters in the field

SCADA can display measurements and alarms from remote sites, but data quality depends on sensor placement, calibration, communications, and alarm design.

A practical example

A steady reading from a failed or isolated sensor is not proof that system pressure is steady.
