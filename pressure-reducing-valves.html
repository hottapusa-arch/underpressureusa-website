Pressure-Reducing Valves

Pressure-reducing valves help control downstream pressure where supply pressure is higher than needed.

← All Pumps & Pressure topics

How it works

These valves regulate pressure under specified operating conditions, often using a pilot-controlled arrangement in larger water systems.

Why it matters

They can protect lower-elevation service areas and help manage leakage and pressure variation.

What can go wrong?

Improper settings, debris, or maintenance problems can lead to unstable pressure or inadequate service.

Remember this

A reducing valve controls pressure; it does not increase the available energy or replace adequate supply.

Control without wasting pressure

A pressure-reducing valve uses a control mechanism to maintain a lower downstream pressure over a defined operating range. It can protect lower-elevation areas and reduce leakage, but incorrect settings or maintenance problems may cause poor service or pressure instability.

Explore the bigger system

Pressure, flow, pumps, storage, and valves all influence one another. See our overview of water distribution systems to understand how the pieces connect.

Educational overview only. System-specific design, operation, and maintenance require applicable standards, approved procedures, and qualified personnel.

What matters in the field

A pressure-reducing valve is used to maintain a lower downstream pressure under appropriate operating conditions. It requires correct sizing, installation, and maintenance.

A practical example

A PRV is not a universal cure for every pressure problem; low-flow conditions and transient events need consideration.

Pressure control is not the same as isolation

A pressure-reducing valve (PRV) is designed to regulate downstream pressure under specified flow conditions. It should not automatically be treated as a tight shutoff valve for maintenance.

What operators watch

Downstream pressure, upstream pressure, flow demand, pilot or control-line condition, and strainer cleanliness can all affect performance. Pressure that creeps upward during low demand may indicate a control or seating problem requiring investigation.
