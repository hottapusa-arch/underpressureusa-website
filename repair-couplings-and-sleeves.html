Repair Couplings & Sleeves

The difference between joining, repairing, and reinforcing a pipeline.

← Explore all valves & fittings

Different purposes

A coupling joins pipe ends. A repair sleeve or clamp may seal a localized defect. A tapping or stopping sleeve serves a different, specialized purpose.

Selection basics

Pipe dimensions, defect type, service pressure, material, and approved product ratings determine what is suitable.

Common misunderstandings

A fitting that fits around a pipe is not automatically rated for every repair, pipe material, or service condition.

Why it matters

Knowing the purpose of each component helps avoid treating every sleeve as the same tool.

What matters in the field

Repair couplings and sleeves can address certain leaks or damaged areas, but suitability depends on the failure, pipe material, geometry, and required restraint.

A practical example

A leak-sealing device is not automatically a structural repair for a pipe with extensive wall loss.

Measure the actual pipe

A repair sleeve or coupling must match the pipe’s real outside diameter and condition, not merely its nominal size. Corrosion, ovality, surface defects, and nearby joints can affect sealing. A repair that stops a leak still needs appropriate restraint and pressure suitability.
