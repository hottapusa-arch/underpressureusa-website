Sewer Force Mains

The pressurized pipelines that carry wastewater away from pumping stations.

← All Sewer Systems topics

Where gravity cannot do the job

A force main carries wastewater under pressure from a pump station to a higher point or another part of the collection system. Unlike a typical gravity sewer, it is intended to run full while pumping, and its pressure changes as pumps start, stop, and flow conditions change.

Pressure and surge considerations

Pipe material, wall class, fittings, thrust restraint, air management, and operating pressure all matter. Rapid pump changes or valve actions can create pressure transients. The design must account for more than the steady pressure shown on a gauge.

Air, gases, and odor

High points can collect air or gas and reduce hydraulic performance. Wastewater can also generate corrosive or hazardous gases, including hydrogen sulfide. Air-release equipment and venting require designs suitable for sewage service and appropriate safety procedures.

Failure signs

Unexpected wet ground, odor, loss of station performance, unusual pump cycling, or changes in discharge pressure may point toward a problem. A leak may not always be obvious at the surface, especially where wastewater follows a trench or other underground path.

Isolation and repair planning

Force-main work requires a plan for shutting down or controlling pumping, managing upstream flow, and preventing a release. Temporary bypass pumping may be necessary when service cannot stop. Crews must verify isolation and follow confined-space and wastewater-exposure procedures where applicable.

Field takeaway

Treat a force main as a pressurized wastewater pipeline, not as a gravity sewer with a pump attached. Know the operating conditions, upstream storage limits, and available bypass options before opening the system.
