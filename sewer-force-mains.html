Sewer Force Mains

The pressurized pipelines that carry wastewater away from pumping stations.

← All Sewer Systems topics

Pressure instead of slope

A force main carries pumped wastewater under pressure. Unlike a gravity sewer, it can follow terrain without relying on a continuous downhill grade.

Materials and fittings

Material selection, joints, bends, valves, and restraint must account for pressure, corrosion conditions, and wastewater service.

Air and gas

Air accumulation and wastewater gases can affect operation. The system may need properly designed air-management equipment and corrosion controls.

Failure considerations

Leaks or breaks can release untreated wastewater. Isolation and repair planning must account for the contents of the pipeline and local response requirements.

What matters in the field

A force main carries wastewater under pressure from a pump station. Air, gases, corrosion, surge behavior, and maintenance access can affect reliability.

A practical example

Force mains share hydraulic principles with water mains but have different contents, operating cycles, and safety concerns.

A sewer under pressure behaves differently

Force mains carry wastewater from pumping stations where gravity flow alone is not practical. Pressure changes, trapped gases, corrosion, and air-release equipment can create distinct maintenance concerns. A force-main failure may not be visible until wastewater surfaces some distance from the break.

A sewer line under pressure

Unlike a gravity sewer, a force main carries wastewater under pressure from a pump or lift station. Materials may include PVC pressure pipe, HDPE, ductile iron, and other engineered systems. Pressure rating, surge, corrosion, air management, and isolation all matter. A force main should not be treated like a gravity sewer during repair or excavation.
