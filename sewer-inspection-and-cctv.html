Sewer Inspection and CCTV

How cameras and condition assessments help identify underground problems.

← All Sewer Systems topics

What a camera inspection can show

Closed-circuit television inspection lets crews see the inside of a gravity sewer without exposing the entire pipe. Footage may reveal cracks, displaced joints, root intrusion, deposits, infiltration, standing water, and other conditions that affect performance.

Start with the reason for inspection

A recurring blockage, suspected collapse, new construction acceptance, or routine condition assessment calls for different observations. Cleaning may be needed before inspection so deposits do not hide defects, but the cleaning method must suit the pipe condition and utility procedures.

Location and measurement matter

Record the inspected segment, upstream and downstream structures, direction of travel, and distance to each observation. Pipe diameter, material, and access conditions help interpret what the camera sees. An apparent defect in footage is less useful if crews cannot locate it for repair.

What CCTV cannot prove by itself

A camera shows visible internal conditions; it does not directly measure every aspect of remaining wall strength or explain all surrounding soil conditions. Water level, poor lighting, debris, and camera orientation can obscure defects. Some findings call for additional investigation before selecting a repair.

Turn footage into a maintenance decision

Use consistent defect coding and retain footage for comparison. Distinguish conditions needing immediate attention from those suitable for monitoring, cleaning, rehabilitation, or planned replacement. Entry into manholes and other confined spaces requires the utility’s applicable safety procedures.
