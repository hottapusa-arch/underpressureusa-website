Why Sewer Pipes Fail

Gravity sewers and force mains fail in different ways.

← All Infrastructure Failures topics

What is happening?

Gravity sewer lines depend on slope and open flow paths. Force mains operate under pressure. Each faces its own failure modes.

Why it happens

Gravity sewers can suffer cracks, joint displacement, root intrusion, sediment buildup, and collapse. Force mains may face corrosion, pressure events, air problems, and leaks.

What crews look for

CCTV inspections, maintenance history, flow observations, and site conditions help identify what is happening. A blockage and a structural failure are not the same problem.

What we can learn

Utilities choose cleaning, point repair, rehabilitation, or replacement based on condition, capacity, and risk.

What matters in the field

Sewer failures may involve structural collapse, root intrusion, joint separation, corrosion, or inadequate capacity. The symptoms can look similar at the surface.

A practical example

A recurring blockage may be caused by pipe deformation rather than debris alone.

Failures are not always collapses

Sewer defects include joint separation, root intrusion, cracks, corrosion, deformation, and loss of grade. Some allow groundwater in long before a complete blockage develops. Inspection history and consequence of failure guide which defects need urgent action.
