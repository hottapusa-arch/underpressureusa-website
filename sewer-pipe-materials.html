Sewer Pipe Materials

Clay, concrete, PVC, ductile iron, and other materials found in sewer infrastructure.

← All Sewer Systems topics

Older networks

Vitrified clay and concrete are common in many older collection systems. Their performance depends on installation, joints, loading, and exposure.

Modern choices

PVC, HDPE, ductile iron, concrete, and other products are selected for different gravity and pressure applications.

Corrosion and abrasion

Wastewater chemistry, hydrogen sulfide conditions, grit, and cleaning methods can influence material performance.

No one material fits every job

Designers consider soil, depth, traffic loads, hydraulics, constructability, service conditions, and local standards.

What matters in the field

Sewer pipe materials include vitrified clay, concrete, PVC, ductile iron, and others. Their joints, corrosion resistance, stiffness, and installation requirements differ.

A practical example

A material that works well in a gravity sewer may not be appropriate for a pressurized force main without a suitable design.

The environment drives material selection

Gravity sewers and force mains face different loads and chemical conditions. Concrete, vitrified clay, PVC, ductile iron, and HDPE each have strengths and limits. Abrasion, hydrogen sulfide corrosion, soil movement, and installation quality affect long-term performance.
