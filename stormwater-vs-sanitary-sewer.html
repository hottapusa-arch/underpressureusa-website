Stormwater vs. Sanitary Sewers

Why drainage systems and wastewater collection systems are not the same.

← All Sewer Systems topics

Sanitary sewers

Sanitary sewers carry wastewater from toilets, sinks, businesses, and other approved connections to treatment.

Storm drains

Storm drainage systems primarily carry rainwater runoff, often to streams or other receiving waters with different treatment arrangements.

Combined sewers

Some older cities have combined systems that carry sanitary wastewater and stormwater in the same pipes. Heavy rain can create special overflow challenges.

Why connections matter

Connecting a sump pump or roof drain to a sanitary sewer where prohibited can overload the system. Local rules vary.

What matters in the field

Storm drains carry runoff; sanitary sewers carry wastewater to treatment. Combined systems exist in some older communities.

A practical example

Connecting stormwater to a sanitary sewer can overwhelm collection and treatment systems during rain.

Two systems with different jobs

Sanitary sewers collect wastewater from homes and businesses for treatment. Stormwater systems move rainfall and runoff, often to receiving waters. Some older communities have combined sewers, which complicates wet-weather operation and overflow control.

Educational overview only. Utility policies, site conditions, safety procedures, and applicable regulations govern field work.
