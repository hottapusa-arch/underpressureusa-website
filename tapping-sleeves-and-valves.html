Tapping Sleeves & Valves

How a new branch can be added to a pressurized water main.

← Explore all valves & fittings

Types of tapping sleeves and saddles

Full-body tapping sleeves surround the main and provide a flanged or threaded outlet for the new connection. Depending on the manufacturer, these can be fabricated steel, ductile iron, cast iron, or stainless steel. Their gaskets, bolts, pressure ratings, and permitted pipe materials differ; full-body does not mean every sleeve seals the same way.

Stainless-steel tapping sleeves are common because they can be made to fit a range of pipe outside diameters. Some are wraparound designs with a broad gasketed seal; others have more localized sealing arrangements. The actual pipe outside diameter, ovality, surface condition, and manufacturer’s approved range determine fit.

Brass or bronze service saddles are generally used for smaller service connections rather than large branch taps. They clamp to the main and provide a threaded outlet for a corporation stop or other approved connection. Other saddle materials include ductile iron and stainless steel. A service saddle and a full-size tapping sleeve serve different jobs and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Mechanical-joint tapping sleeves and other specialty designs also exist for particular applications. Some products are intended for a particular pipe material or outside-diameter family. A sleeve that fits one nominal size of PVC may not fit the same nominal size of cast iron or asbestos-cement pipe.

Matching the sleeve, valve, and main

For a full-size pressure tap, the tapping gate valve must match the sleeve outlet, flange, pressure rating, and tapping machine arrangement. The valve needs a clear waterway for the cutter and coupon. A standard butterfly valve is not a substitute for the tapping gate valve used in this arrangement.

A sleeve may be suitable for potable water, wastewater force mains, or other compatible fluids, but material selection and approvals depend on the fluid, temperature, pressure, and governing utility requirements. Never assume that a product approved for one service is approved for another.

The sleeve-to-pipe seal, sleeve-to-valve connection, and valve body and seals are all part of the pressure boundary. Testing the assembled sleeve and valve together helps evaluate those possible leak paths before the main is opened.

What matters in the field

A tapping sleeve provides the pressure-containing connection to a main; a purpose-built tapping gate valve provides a clear path for the cutter. Compatibility matters.

A practical example

On compatible tapping assemblies, matching male and female alignment features help center the valve on the sleeve outlet.
