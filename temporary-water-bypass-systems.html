Temporary Water Bypass Systems

How crews keep service available while a section of main is out of operation.

← All Field Operations topics

Why use a bypass?

A temporary bypass routes water around a work zone or isolated pipe section. It may be used during repairs, replacement, or line stopping.

Sizing and demand

The bypass must support expected flow and pressure, including any special needs established by the utility. A hose that works at low demand may not meet peak conditions.

Protecting water quality

Approved potable-water materials, backflow protection, flushing, disinfection, and monitoring may be necessary depending on the arrangement.

Site realities

Traffic, freezing weather, hose protection, connections, and emergency access can shape the design as much as the pipe itself.

What matters in the field

A temporary bypass can maintain supply while a main is isolated for repair or replacement. Capacity, pressure, water quality, protection, and customer connections must be considered.

A practical example

A bypass that delivers enough water for normal use may still need separate evaluation for peak demand or fire-flow requirements.

When temporary storage is needed

A bypass hose moves water around a work area. When a permanent storage tank is out of service, a trailer-mounted hydropneumatic tank may also be used to provide temporary storage and pressure support. Learn about temporary water storage and hydropneumatic trailers.
