Temporary Water Storage: Trailer-Mounted Hydropneumatic Tanks

Keeping water available while permanent storage tanks are repaired, maintained, or replaced.

← Explore all valves & fittings

Why bring in temporary storage

A permanent water storage tank may need to be taken offline for inspection, recoating, structural repairs, cleaning, or replacement. A temporary hydropneumatic tank can be transported to the site on a trailer to help maintain service while the permanent tank is out of operation. The trailer is simply the means of transporting and supporting the tank. It is the tank—not the trailer—that is connected to the water system through appropriate piping and controls. The temporary arrangement must be designed around the system it supports rather than assumed to be a direct replacement for the original tank.

What a hydropneumatic tank does

A hydropneumatic tank stores water beneath a compressed air cushion. As water is drawn out, the compressed air helps maintain delivery pressure. A pump or incoming supply refills the tank, and a compressor or other approved air-control arrangement maintains the required air cushion. Tank size alone does not tell you how much usable water can be delivered between pressure limits.

How the temporary tank connects to the water system

The trailer transports and supports the hydropneumatic tank at the jobsite. After the trailer is positioned and secured, the tank’s inlet and outlet are connected to the water system through approved piping, hoses, valves, and isolation points. The trailer itself is not part of the water connection. Connection details, water quality safeguards, and operating limits must be approved for the particular system.

Pressure and level are different measurements

A tank can show acceptable pressure while having limited usable water remaining. Operators need to understand both water level and pressure, as well as whether the supply is refilling fast enough. Low-water protection, high-pressure protection, alarms, and continuous or remote monitoring can help crews identify a problem before customers lose service.

Water quality and site conditions

Temporary potable-water storage requires appropriate tank materials, cleaning, disinfection, sanitary connections, backflow protection where required, and a monitoring plan. Freezing temperatures, hose routing, vehicle traffic, security, and access to the tank and its controls also matter. The trailer must be safely positioned and secured so the mounted tank and its piping can operate as intended. Tanks used for wastewater or other fluids require different compatibility and safety considerations; they are not automatically interchangeable with potable-water equipment.

Not the same as a bypass hose

A temporary bypass typically routes flow around a section of pipe. Temporary storage adds a reservoir and, with hydropneumatic equipment, a way to help maintain pressure. The two approaches may be used together, but they solve different problems. A properly planned temporary storage system can give crews time to work on the permanent tank without unnecessarily interrupting service.
