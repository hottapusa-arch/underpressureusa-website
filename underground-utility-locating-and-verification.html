Underground Utility Locating & Verification

How records, locating tools, and physical verification work together before excavation.

← All Field Operations topics

A mark is not a guarantee

Underground utility maps are starting points, not exact pictures of what is buried. A facility may have been moved, abandoned, repaired, or recorded with limited accuracy. Surface paint and flags identify approximate locations under the applicable locating process; they do not establish exact depth or prove that no other utility exists.

Start with the required notification

Before digging, contact the applicable one-call notification center within the required advance notice period and follow state law, ticket requirements, and utility-owner instructions. In North Carolina, NC 811 is the notification system. Private facilities may not be covered by public locating services, so identify who must locate them separately. Maintain the ticket and marks as required while the work proceeds.

What locating equipment can tell you

Electromagnetic locators detect signals on conductive utilities or tracer wires. They can be effective when a usable signal path exists, but nearby metal, bonded utilities, depth, and interference can confuse the result. Ground-penetrating radar can detect contrasts below the surface under favorable soil and site conditions; it does not identify every pipe or reliably determine its contents. Neither method should be treated as infallible.

Directional drilling and other boring

Horizontal directional drilling, auger boring, and similar trenchless work can strike facilities without a visible open trench. Locate and positively verify crossings and critical clearances along the planned bore path. Account for the bore diameter, tooling, alignment tolerance, and the potential for the path to change. A pilot bore is not proof of clearance for a larger reamer.

Expose the utility when necessary

At crossings or other critical locations, use an approved non-destructive exposure method such as vacuum excavation or careful hand digging within the required tolerance zone. Confirm the facility’s position and depth before mechanical excavation or drilling proceeds. Protect exposed pipes and cables from movement, undermining, and equipment contact.

The field takeaway

Use records to plan, locating methods to narrow the search, and physical verification where the risk demands certainty. Stop and resolve conflicting information rather than forcing the excavation to match the map.
