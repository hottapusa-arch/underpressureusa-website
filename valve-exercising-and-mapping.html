Valve Exercising & Mapping

Why knowing where a valve is matters as much as knowing what it does.

← Explore all valves & fittings

The hidden problem

A valve may be buried, mislabeled, paved over, or recorded inaccurately. Even a working valve cannot help if crews cannot find it.

Exercising and maintenance

Utilities use planned valve-maintenance programs to learn whether valves operate and to identify problems before an emergency. Procedures should reflect valve type and condition.

What records should capture

Location, size, direction of operation, condition, observed response, and maintenance history can all help future crews.

The bigger lesson

System isolation depends on good records, maintained equipment, and an understanding of how mains connect.

What matters in the field

Exercising helps operators assess valve condition and maintain familiarity with operation. Mapping links each valve to the customers and mains it controls.

A practical example

A valve that turns fully may still pass water; successful isolation requires more than counting turns.

A valve that turns may still not seal

Valve exercising helps utilities identify seized operators, damaged stems, buried boxes, and valves that do not reach their expected positions. Mapping records the actual location and operating direction. A maintenance program also tracks whether a valve provides effective isolation, not just whether it can be turned.
