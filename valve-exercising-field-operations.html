Valve Exercising in the Field

Why routine valve operation matters before an emergency.

← All Field Operations topics

The purpose

Valve exercising programs help utilities locate valves, assess their condition, and keep them ready for isolation when needed.

Neglect has consequences

Valves left untouched for years may be hard to operate, buried, or damaged. Exercising can reveal problems before a water main break.

Operation takes judgment

Crews should know the valve type, expected turns, operating direction, and system conditions. Forcing a valve can damage it, and rapid changes can disturb system pressure.

Document the result

Good records capture location, accessibility, condition, operation, and follow-up work. That information becomes valuable during the next emergency.

What matters in the field

Field exercising programs document valve location, operation, condition, and unusual resistance. They also create opportunities to correct records.

A practical example

Forcing a stuck valve can cause damage. Utility procedures and valve condition should govern the response.

Slow and deliberate matters

Older valves may have damaged stems, deposits, or years of neglected maintenance. Excessive torque can make a bad situation worse. Field crews follow utility procedures, document turns and condition, and investigate valves that behave differently from expectations.
