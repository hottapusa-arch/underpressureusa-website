Valve Insertion in the Field

Adding isolation capability to an existing water main.

← All Field Operations topics

Designed for installation under pressure

Valve-insertion systems are designed to install a permanent working valve into an existing pressurized main. That is the point of the technology: add a future shutdown location without first shutting down the line being modified. Actual installation still depends on the approved manufacturer system, pipe condition, pressure, and jobsite requirements.

Every system uses a sleeve and a cartridge or bonnet assembly, but manufacturers create the final shutoff seal in different ways. Some remove the top portion of the existing pipe and seal the closing element against the remaining lower pipe wall. Others remove an entire section of pipe and seal the valve within its own body or assembly. The differences affect fit, sealing surfaces, and how the finished valve operates.

The result is a permanent, operable isolation valve, not a temporary line stop. After installation, the utility can close that valve for future repairs, maintenance, or planned shutdowns, subject to its design and condition.

Why add a valve?

An existing main may have too few working isolation valves. Adding one can reduce the number of customers affected by future repairs.

The basic assembly

Valve insertion systems use a sleeve installed around the existing pipe and a cartridge or bonnet assembly that creates the new valve opening and closure. The details vary by manufacturer and system.

Planning ahead

Crews evaluate pipe material, outside diameter, condition, nearby fittings, and how the valve will be operated and maintained later.

What matters in the field

Valve insertion creates a permanent control point using a pressure-containing sleeve and an insertion system with a cartridge or bonnet assembly.

A practical example

The installation method and tooling vary by manufacturer, so crews must understand the specific system rather than assume every insert works identically.

The permanent result is the key

Valve insertion uses a pressure-containing sleeve and a cartridge or bonnet assembly to create a new permanent isolation point in an existing main. The specific equipment sequence varies by system, but the goal is a valve the utility can operate after the installation crew leaves.

What must be checked in the field

Pipe material, actual outside diameter, ovality, wall condition, coatings, nearby fittings, and available working room all matter. So do the manufacturer’s pressure and application limits. The crew must understand which parts remain permanently in the system and how the finished valve will be accessed and exercised.
