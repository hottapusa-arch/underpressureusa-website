Wastewater Treatment Basics

What happens after wastewater leaves the collection system.

← All Sewer Systems topics

Preliminary treatment

Screens and grit removal protect downstream equipment by removing larger debris and abrasive material.

Biological treatment

Many plants use microorganisms to break down dissolved and suspended organic material under controlled conditions.

Solids handling

Treatment creates residual solids that require additional processing and appropriate disposal or beneficial use.

Disinfection and discharge

Depending on permits and treatment goals, facilities may disinfect treated effluent before discharge or reuse.

What matters in the field

Wastewater treatment commonly uses screening, solids removal, biological processes, clarification, and disinfection where required. The process varies by facility and permit.

A practical example

Removing visible solids is only one part of treatment; dissolved and microscopic pollutants require additional processes.

Treatment protects receiving waters

Wastewater treatment commonly separates solids, uses biological processes to reduce pollutants, and may disinfect before discharge. Sludge handling and nutrient removal can be major parts of the process. The collection system affects treatment performance because infiltration and industrial discharges change incoming flows and loads.

Educational overview only. Utility policies, site conditions, safety procedures, and applicable regulations govern field work.
