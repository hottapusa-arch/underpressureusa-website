Water Main Fittings

Tees, bends, reducers, couplings, and the connections between pipe sections.

← Explore all valves & fittings

Why fittings exist

Pipelines change direction, branch, transition in diameter, and connect to equipment. Fittings make those transitions possible.

Common types

Bends change direction; tees create branches; reducers change size; couplings join pipe sections. Each has its own intended application.

Pressure forces

Changes in direction and diameter create forces that the system must resist through appropriate restraint or thrust control.

Fit matters

Pipe outside diameter, material, pressure class, joint type, and manufacturer requirements all matter when selecting a fitting.

What matters in the field

Fittings connect, change direction, branch, or terminate pipe. Outside diameter, pressure rating, joint type, and restraint must match the actual application.

A practical example

A fitting that slides onto a pipe is not necessarily correctly rated or restrained for the service.

Fittings carry forces too

Bends, tees, reducers, and dead ends change the direction or magnitude of water pressure forces. Thrust restraint or blocking may be required depending on design. Fitting compatibility also depends on actual pipe dimensions, material, pressure rating, and joint type.
