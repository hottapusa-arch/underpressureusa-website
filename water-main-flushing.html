Water Main Flushing

How controlled flushing supports distribution-system maintenance.

← All Field Operations topics

Why utilities flush water mains

Water moving through a distribution system can carry fine particles, and some areas experience low turnover. Flushing intentionally moves water through selected mains to help manage deposits, address water age, or support maintenance and water-quality work. It is not simply opening the nearest hydrant and letting water run.

What happens when flow changes

Normal daily demand may move water in one direction at a relatively low velocity. Opening a hydrant or other outlet changes the flow pattern. Depending on the available supply and network configuration, the higher velocity can lift loose material from the pipe and carry it toward the discharge point. That material may include sediment or corrosion products.

Routine versus directional flushing

Routine flushing may be used to circulate water or investigate water-quality concerns. Unidirectional flushing is a more controlled approach: crews use selected valves and outlets to direct flow through a planned section of main. The objective is to produce an effective cleaning velocity and move material out of the system without simply pushing it into another neighborhood.

Why customers sometimes see discolored water

A sudden change in flow can stir up deposits and temporarily discolor water. Discoloration has more than one possible cause, so it should not automatically be dismissed as harmless sediment. Customers should follow their utility’s guidance and report persistent or unusual changes in water appearance, taste, or odor.

What planning has to account for

Hydrant operation affects pressure and flow elsewhere in the network. Utilities consider available water, pressure at critical customers, traffic, drainage, environmental discharge requirements, and how valves are positioned. Flushing a main with the wrong valve configuration can send material in an unintended direction.

The lesson beneath the street

Flushing shows that water distribution is a connected system. One open outlet can change conditions far from where the work is happening. Our Water Pressure Fundamentals guide explains why pressure, velocity, friction, and system layout matter when flow changes.
