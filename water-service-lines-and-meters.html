Service Lines and Water Meters

The final connection between a water main and a building matters.

← All Water Systems topics

The final connection to the customer

A water service branches from a distribution main and delivers water toward a property. Depending on local design, the connection may include a corporation stop, service tubing, curb stop or meter stop, meter, and backflow protection where required. Service materials and layouts vary widely by age and utility.

Where the responsibility changes

Ownership and maintenance responsibility are established by the utility and local rules. The meter, property line, curb stop, and building wall are not universal responsibility boundaries. Before a repair, verify which section the utility maintains and which section belongs to the property owner.

Why meter readings matter

A meter measures usage, but its reading alone does not locate a leak. Unexpected continuous consumption can suggest a customer-side problem, while water surfacing near the street may involve the utility service or main. Leak investigations often combine meter observations, isolation checks, acoustic methods, and site conditions.

Materials and aging services

Older services may use lead, galvanized steel, copper, or other legacy materials; newer work may use approved copper or plastic tubing. Material identification matters for compatibility, corrosion, pressure rating, and public-health requirements. Lead-service-line inventories and replacement programs are subject to current drinking-water regulations and utility procedures.

What crews verify in the field

Before exposing or repairing a service, crews confirm the main location, service route, utility ownership, other buried facilities, and the intended isolation point. Connections must use fittings and methods approved for the pipe material and system pressure. Restoring service may also require flushing, disinfection, testing, or customer notification.

The practical takeaway

Small-diameter services can create big customer impacts. Accurate records, sound connections, and a clear understanding of ownership are just as important here as on large mains.

Educational overview only. Follow utility policies, approved procedures, and applicable regulations for field work.
