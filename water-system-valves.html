Valves in Water Systems

Valves help utilities control flow and isolate sections for work.

← All Water Systems topics

Different jobs

Isolation valves, check valves, air valves, and pressure-control valves perform different functions. A valve selected for one purpose may not suit another.

Isolation planning

Crews use system maps and field verification to identify the valves that could isolate a repair area while limiting customer impacts.

Condition matters

A valve that has not been operated in years may not perform as expected. Inspection, records, and planned exercising programs help utilities understand their systems.

Different valves do different jobs

Gate and butterfly valves commonly provide isolation, check valves prevent reverse flow, and pressure-control valves regulate operating conditions. No single valve type fits every task. Selection depends on flow, pressure, size, access, and maintenance needs.

Explore the bigger system

Water sources, treatment, storage, distribution, and maintenance are connected. Read our water distribution overview or continue to Fire Hydrants and Fire Flow.

Educational overview only. Local operating rules, utility policies, and applicable standards govern real-world work.

What matters in the field

Isolation, regulation, backflow prevention, and air management are different valve functions. Choosing a valve begins with the job it must perform.

A practical example

A valve that is excellent for throttling is not automatically appropriate as a tapping valve.
